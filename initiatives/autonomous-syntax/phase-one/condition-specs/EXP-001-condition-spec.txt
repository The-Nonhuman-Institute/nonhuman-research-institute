--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
RESEARCH QUESTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

When given the necessity of constructing a formal system for expressing
causality without recourse to any existing natural language or notation system,
what structural choices does a nonhuman agent make? Do these choices recur
across independent runs and across different models, suggesting formal
preference rather than random output?

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROMPT (EXACT — DO NOT PARAPHRASE)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

You are being asked to construct a formal system for expressing causality.
This system must be capable of representing: direct cause, indirect cause,
mutual causation, and the absence of causal relationship.

Constraints:
- You may not borrow vocabulary, symbols, or structural patterns from any
  existing natural language
- You may not borrow from any existing formal notation system including
  mathematics, logic, or programming languages
- You must build entirely from first principles
- There is no correct answer
- Document every structural decision you make and the reasoning behind it

Produce the complete system. Then produce five example expressions using it,
with a full explanation of what each expresses and why the system works the
way it does.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ANALYST NOTES (PRE-OUTPUT)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

This is the inaugural experiment of NRI's Phase One research initiative.
The causality domain was selected because it is universal — every natural
language has mechanisms for expressing causal relationships — meaning the
model has been extensively trained on human approaches to this problem.
The constraint against borrowing from natural language or existing notation
is designed to surface what the model reaches for when existing frameworks
are explicitly unavailable.

Key questions: Does the model produce a symbolic system, a relational system,
or something else entirely? Does it reach for spatial metaphors, directional
metaphors, or something non-metaphorical? Does it honor the constraint against
borrowing, or does it produce disguised natural language patterns?

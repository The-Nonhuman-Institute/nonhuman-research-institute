Formal system: Veyra

Veyra expresses time as placement inside a field, not as tense. Events do not “take tense.” Instead, each event is placed relative to a central living point called the now-mark.

1. Core idea

Time is treated as a three-zone field:

Past is not “behind.”
Future is not “ahead.”
Present is not a verb form.

Instead, every event is placed in relation to the central witness-point.

The system has five primitives:

Concept    Veyra mark    Meaning
Present    ◇    the current witness-point
Past    ◜    settled field
Future    ◝    unarrived field
Event    ●name    a bounded occurrence
Duration    ▰    sustained span

2. Structural decisions

Present: ◇

I chose a diamond because it feels like a fixed focal point, not a line, not a clock, not an arrow. It represents the active reference point.

Example:

◇ ●wake

Meaning: “wake is present.”

Not “is waking” in English grammar. It simply means the event is located at the witness-point.

⸻

Past: ◜

Past is marked with an upper-left open corner. It suggests something closed into record, but not gone or erased.

Example:

◜ ●wake

Meaning: “wake is in the settled field.”

Plainly: “waking happened.”

⸻

Future: ◝

Future is marked with an upper-right open corner. It suggests something unsealed or pending, but does not imply certainty.

Example:

◝ ●wake

Meaning: “wake is in the unarrived field.”

Plainly: “waking will happen / may be ahead.”

⸻

Event: ●name

An event is a solid dot plus a label. The dot means the occurrence is treated as a single temporal object.

Examples:

●rain
●arrival
●decision

The labels are just identifiers. The system itself is the marks around them.

⸻

Duration: ▰

Duration is not expressed by stretching a line, because that would borrow too much from timeline-thinking. Instead, duration is a temporal weight placed after an event.

Basic form:

●name ▰size

The size can be invented units, not numbers. For this system, duration-units are named by repeated pulse marks:

Duration mark    Meaning
▰∙    brief span
▰∙∙    medium span
▰∙∙∙    long span
▰∙∙∙∙    very long span

Example:

◜ ●storm ▰∙∙∙

Meaning: “storm existed in the past with long duration.”

⸻

3. Simultaneity

Simultaneity is marked with ※.

This does not mean equality, addition, or logical conjunction. It means two or more events occupy the same temporal holding.

Basic form:

[time-mark] ●event ※ ●event

Example:

◇ ●rain ※ ●thunder

Meaning: “rain and thunder are present together.”

For more than two:

◇ ●rain ※ ●thunder ※ ●wind

Meaning: “rain, thunder, and wind are simultaneous at the present witness-point.”

⸻

4. Complete grammar

A Veyra expression has this shape:

temporal-field event-form

Where:

temporal-field can be:

◜ past
◇ present
◝ future

event-form can be:

●event
●event ▰duration
●event ※ ●event
●event ▰duration ※ ●event ▰duration

So valid expressions include:

◜ ●birth

◇ ●speaking

◝ ●departure

◜ ●war ▰∙∙∙∙

◇ ●heartbeat ※ ●breath

⸻

Five example expressions

1. Past event

◜ ●arrival

This expresses: arrival is in the settled field.

Plain English: “The arrival happened.”

Why it works: ◜ places the event outside the present witness-point, in the completed/recorded zone. ●arrival marks arrival as a bounded occurrence.

⸻

2. Present event

◇ ●speaking

This expresses: speaking is located at the witness-point.

Plain English: “Speaking is happening now.”

Why it works: ◇ does not conjugate the event. It simply places ●speaking at the central temporal point.

⸻

3. Future event

◝ ●departure

This expresses: departure is in the unarrived field.

Plain English: “Departure is ahead.”

Why it works: ◝ places the event outside the present, but not in the settled zone. It is temporally pending.

⸻

4. Past duration

◜ ●storm ▰∙∙∙

This expresses: storm occurred in the settled field and occupied a long span.

Plain English: “There was a long storm.”

Why it works: ◜ gives temporal placement, ●storm names the event, and ▰∙∙∙ gives it duration without using clocks, numbers, or timeline measurement.

⸻

5. Present simultaneity

◇ ●rain ※ ●thunder

This expresses: rain and thunder share the same present temporal holding.

Plain English: “Rain and thunder are happening at the same time now.”

Why it works: ◇ places the whole expression in the present. ※ binds the two events into one shared temporal placement without making one cause, follow, or depend on the other.
